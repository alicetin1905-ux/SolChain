How wallets are judged

  • Candidates. Your watchlist, the leaderboard's best non-bot wallets, the repeat early buyers, the wallets the engine follows now and last run's picks.
  • History. Up to 250 of each wallet's most recent transactions from the last 30 days are read and its swaps worked out per token. Very active wallets therefore cover fewer days.
  • Checks. A wallet is copied only when it traded at least 5 tokens, at least 30% of its copies would have made money, the copy backtest is positive, it makes no more than 150 transactions a day, and it usually holds a token for at least 60 seconds.
  • Copying. The engine follows the 10 best that pass, by backtest, plus up to 5 early buyers that pass. It drops a wallet whose live copies keep losing.
  • Limits. The backtest uses the wallet's own prices plus 3% slippage, not the price a copier would really have got a few seconds later.
